[155] [Body Text] In rapidly urbanizing cities like Pune, road congestion, poorly maintained infrastructure, and inefficient management of public spaces are recurring challenges that significantly affect daily commuting. The lack of accessible systems for reporting infrastructure issues, such as potholes or traffic hotspots, leads to delays in repairs and contributes to unsafe travel conditions. This issue is further exacerbated by unplanned events or gatherings on roadways, as well as sudden traffic surges around educational institutions during opening and closing times. Without a centralized system to report, analyze, and address these factors, both commuters and city authorities struggle with unpredictable traffic patterns and infrastructure strain, impacting the quality of life and urban mobility.
[156] [Body Text] 
[157] [Body Text] 
[158] [Body Text] 
[159] [Body Text] 
[160] [Body Text] The recurring issue faced by commuters and local authorities is the absence of an integrated platform to systematically report and analyze road conditions, events, and traffic influencers in real time. Citizens have limited channels to report potholes, infrastructure complaints, or event permissions, resulting in delayed responses and poor road conditions. Additionally, there is a growing demand for a route-analysis tool that considers multiple factors influencing traffic beyond just live congestion data. Without such a system, both citizens and municipal authorities lack actionable insights into road safety, traffic efficiency, and maintenance needs, which ultimately leads to inefficient urban management, unpredictable delays, and compromised road safety.
[161] [Body Text] 
[162] [Body Text] 
[163] [Body Text] 
[164] [Body Text] 
[165] [Body Text] The PMC Traffic Analysis Portal addresses these challenges by providing interactive platform for real-time traffic analysis and infrastructure reporting. The system empowers citizens to report potholes and road complaints via image uploads, with issues verified through a machine learning model, and to request event space permissions in specific road-adjacent areas. Its "Analyze Route" feature offers a comprehensive view of route conditions, incorporating data such as traffic status, potholes, complaints, events, and the presence of nearby schools or colleges with opening and closing times close to the current time. By providing a traffic score and actionable insights on route safety and efficiency, the portal not only enhances the commuting experience for users but also supports city authorities in proactively managing road maintenance and traffic control, thereby contributing to safer, more sustainable urban mobility.

[205] [List Paragraph] PURPOSE	
[206] [Body Text] 
[207] [Body Text] The purpose of this traffic analysis project is to develop an efficient solution for monitoring urban traffic flow, road safety, and congestion in rapidly growing cities like Pune. Existing traffic management solutions often lack the ability to address location-specific issues, such as road hazards, unplanned events, and peak congestion near schools and colleges. Our goal is to provide an affordable, user-friendly platform that leverages real-time data to improve route planning and offer actionable insights for traffic management and road safety.

[214] [Body Text] 
[215] [Body Text] 					
[216] [Normal] Table 2.2.1: Literature review
[217] [Body Text] 
[218] [Body Text] Currently, road issue reporting and traffic analysis are managed through separate, often limited, systems that lack integration and accessibility. Citizens face difficulties in reporting infrastructure issues, resulting in delayed road maintenance and unresolved complaints. Additionally, existing platforms do not provide comprehensive route analysis considering factors like nearby schools, events, potholes, and traffic hotspots. This gap limits citizens’ ability to make well-informed commuting decisions and creates challenges for city authorities in maintaining road infrastructure efficiently.
[219] [Body Text] 
[220] [Body Text] 
[221] [List Paragraph] SYSTEM OBJECTIVES	
[222] [Body Text] 
[223] [Body Text] 1. Facilitate Issue Reporting: Provide a platform for users to report road-related issues such as potholes and other infrastructure concerns through image uploads, allowing for quick verification using a machine learning model. 
[224] [Body Text] 
[225] [Body Text] 2. Streamline Event Registration: Allow citizens to book event spaces alongside roads and public areas with local authority approval to ensure organized use of public space without disrupting traffic.
[226] [Body Text] 
[227] [Body Text] 3. Dual-Map Interface for Comprehensive Data: Implement two map interfaces, one displaying live traffic updates and another for viewing the reported complaints, potholes, events, and nearby institutions—offering a detailed view of road conditions for better decision-making.
[228] [Body Text] 
[229] [Body Text] 4. *Route Analysis with Multiple Factors*: Provide an “Analyze Route” feature that assesses routes based on factors like reported potholes, complaints, events, nearby traffic hotspots, and institutions with opening and closing times close to the current time, as these contribute to peak traffic levels.
[230] [Body Text] 
[231] [Body Text] 5. Traffic Score Calculation: Develop a scoring mechanism to calculate a “traffic score” based on various parameters, such as number of potholes, complaints, traffic hotspots, events, and educational institution nearby falling close to the selected route, to provide users with an understanding of route safety and efficiency.
[232] [Body Text] 
[233] [List Paragraph] ISSUES	
[234] [Body Text] 
[235] [List Paragraph] Existing road issue reporting and traffic information systems are fragmented and lack centralized access, making them inconvenient for citizens.
[236] [List Paragraph] Unresolved road complaints and potholes contribute to unsafe road conditions and higher maintenance costs for municipal authorities.
[237] [List Paragraph] Event permissions and other road space registrations are often handled manually, leading to inefficiencies and potential overlaps.
[238] [List Paragraph] Route analysis is limited to traffic congestion data, without consideration of local factors like school traffic, potholes, and hotspots, reducing the accuracy and usefulness of travel insights.
[239] [Body Text] 
[240] [List Paragraph] ASSUMPTIONS AND CONSTRAINTS	
[241] [Body Text] 
[242] [Body Text] Assumptions
[243] [Body Text] 
[244] [Body Text] Citizens and authorities will have access to an internet-enabled device for using the platform.
[245] [Body Text] Real-time location and traffic data can be reliably accessed and updated on the portal.
[246] [Body Text] 
[247] [Body Text]        Constraints
[248] [Body Text] 
[249] [Body Text] Accurate route analysis depends on timely and correct data inputs from both citizens and city authorities.
[250] [Body Text] Integration of ML-based image verification for pothole reporting may increase system complexity.
[251] [Body Text] High traffic volumes and concurrent users could impact system performance during peak hours.
[252] [Body Text] 
[253] [List Paragraph] ALTERNATIVES	
[254] [Body Text] 
[255] [Body Text] While the PMC Traffic Analysis Portal offers a unique combination of reporting, analysis, and route planning features, there are existing systems and methods that partially address similar needs:
[256] [Body Text] 
[257] [Body Text] Standalone Complaint Management Systems: Existing municipal complaint apps that allow citizens to report road issues.
[258] [Body Text] Traffic Analysis Apps: Tools like Google Maps that provide live traffic updates without specific route analysis features.
[259] [Body Text] Event Management Systems: Platforms for road-event registration that are separate from road safety and reporting systems.
