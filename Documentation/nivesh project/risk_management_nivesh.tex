\section{Problem Statement}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Identify potential risks and prepare mitigation strategies for the \textbf{CA Firm Attendance and Article Management System} --- a software development project. The exercise involves categorizing risks by type, assessing their probability and impact, constructing a risk matrix, and defining proactive mitigation and contingency plans for each identified risk.

% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Introduction}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Risk management is a systematic process of identifying, analyzing, and responding to uncertainties that may adversely affect a software project's scope, schedule, cost, or quality. For the \textbf{CA Firm Attendance and Article Management System} --- a web platform built on React.js, Node.js/Express, and MySQL that digitizes article trainee management through biometric face recognition, GPS-based geofencing, task assignment, and automated reporting --- risk management is critical given the sensitive biometric data involved, real-time camera/GPS dependencies, and role-specific access requirements.

The risk management process for this project follows four steps:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep, label=\roman*)]
    \item \textbf{Risk Identification} --- Enumerate all potential risks across technical, project, and external dimensions.
    \item \textbf{Risk Analysis} --- Assess each risk's probability and impact on a 1--5 scale.
    \item \textbf{Risk Prioritization} --- Classify as High, Medium, or Low using a risk matrix.
    \item \textbf{Risk Mitigation \& Contingency} --- Define proactive and reactive response strategies.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Risk Mitigation Strategies}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{High-Priority Risks (RS $\geq$ 15)}

\subsubsection*{TR-1: Face Recognition Failure (RS = 16)}
\textbf{Probability:} High (4) \quad \textbf{Impact:} High (4)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Set a minimum Euclidean distance threshold of 0.6 (face-api.js) tuned during testing on diverse face samples.
    \item Provide a well-lit environment guidance message and re-capture prompt before denying attendance.
    \item Register multiple face samples per article trainee (front, slight left, slight right angles) during onboarding.
    \item Require a minimum camera resolution of 720p for face recognition to function.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
If face recognition fails three consecutive times for a verified user, Admin can manually mark attendance with a supervisor override code. Log all override cases for audit purposes.

\bigskip
\subsubsection*{TR-2: GPS Inaccuracy / Drift (RS = 16)}
\textbf{Probability:} High (4) \quad \textbf{Impact:} High (4)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Make the geofencing radius configurable by Admin (default: 100m). This allows SPCM to adjust if the office building causes consistent GPS drift.
    \item Wait for GPS accuracy $< 50$m before using the coordinate (reject coordinates with high uncertainty).
    \item Use the Browser Geolocation API's \texttt{enableHighAccuracy: true} option for better precision.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
Admin can configure an expanded geofence radius (up to 300m) if consistent false denials are reported. Persistent GPS failures are logged and reviewable for manual approval.

\bigskip
\subsubsection*{TR-3: HTTPS Not Configured (RS = 15)}
\textbf{Probability:} Medium (3) \quad \textbf{Impact:} Critical (5)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Make HTTPS mandatory from day one of deployment (Render.com and Vercel both enforce HTTPS by default).
    \item During development, use \texttt{localhost} (which browsers treat as a secure context) for camera and GPS testing.
    \item Include HTTPS configuration in the deployment checklist; block production release without it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
If HTTPS cannot be configured on a custom domain immediately, use the platform's default HTTPS subdomain (e.g., \texttt{*.render.com}) as the production URL until the SSL certificate is issued.

\bigskip
\subsubsection*{PR-1: Schedule Slippage (RS = 16)}
\textbf{Probability:} High (4) \quad \textbf{Impact:} High (4)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Allocate 1.5 weeks' buffer at the deployment/documentation phase in the 14-week Gantt plan.
    \item Run T4 (Backend) and T6 (Frontend core) in parallel to compress the schedule.
    \item Lock feature scope at the SRS level; any new feature requires written approval from the project guide.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
De-prioritize Phase 2 features (AI performance analysis, React Native mobile app). Deliver the core web system on time and document deferred features in the TBD list.

\bigskip
\subsubsection*{PR-2: Team Unavailability During Exams (RS = 15)}
\textbf{Probability:} Very High (5) \quad \textbf{Impact:} Medium (3)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Schedule low-effort tasks (documentation, DB schema, testing scripts) during examination weeks.
    \item Front-load high-complexity tasks (biometric integration, backend APIs) before exam season begins.
    \item Non-critical tasks T3, T6 carry 1--2 weeks of float specifically reserved for schedule flexibility.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
Use the 1.5-week buffer in T10 (Deployment \& Documentation) for post-exam catch-up. The team lead coordinates a 3-day intensive sprint after exams conclude.

\bigskip
\subsubsection*{BR-1: Biometric Data Privacy Risk (RS = 15)}
\textbf{Probability:} Medium (3) \quad \textbf{Impact:} Critical (5)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Store only face descriptor vectors (numerical arrays), never raw face images, in the database.
    \item Encrypt face descriptor data at rest using AES-256 encryption in MySQL.
    \item Display an explicit biometric data consent notice to each article trainee during onboarding.
    \item Provide a data deletion workflow: when an article account is deactivated, all biometric data is purged.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
If a privacy complaint is raised, immediately suspend biometric attendance and revert to a manual check-in log while a legal/compliance review is conducted with SPCM.

\bigskip
\subsubsection*{BR-2: Attendance Spoofing via Photo (RS = 16)}
\textbf{Probability:} High (4) \quad \textbf{Impact:} High (4)

\textbf{Proactive Mitigation:}
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
    \item Use face-api.js's live detection capability (blinking/motion detection) to distinguish a live face from a static photo.
    \item Apply a confidence threshold of $\leq 0.6$ Euclidean distance (tight matching) to reject lower-quality matches typical of printed photos.
    \item Combine dual-factor verification: face match \textbf{AND} GPS geofence --- a photo presented from outside the office will still fail the GPS check.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Contingency Plan:}
Log all attendance attempts with the face confidence score and GPS coordinates. Admin can investigate anomalous patterns (e.g., very high confidence score from outside the office geofence).

\section{Conclusion}
% ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This assignment identified 17 potential risks across technical, project, and business/external categories for the CA Firm Attendance and Article Management System. Risk analysis identified seven high-priority risks (RS $\geq$ 15): face recognition failure (TR-1), GPS inaccuracy (TR-2), missing HTTPS configuration (TR-3), schedule slippage (PR-1), team unavailability (PR-2), biometric data privacy (BR-1), and attendance spoofing (BR-2).

The mitigation strategies leverage the system's own dual-factor verification architecture (face {\footnotesize AND} GPS) as a built-in anti-spoofing measure, while data privacy risks are mitigated through descriptor-only storage, encryption at rest, and explicit consent workflows. Project risks are addressed through Agile time-boxing, task parallelization, and a formally frozen SRS signed by SPCM.

The structured risk monitoring plan --- featuring weekly team reviews, bi-weekly sponsor check-ins, and automated attendance log auditing --- ensures risks are detected and responded to early, supporting on-time delivery of the system within the 14-week development window.
